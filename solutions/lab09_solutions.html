4 Precision, Recall, F1

There are other measures of evaluation in addition to accuracy. Common ones in machine learning research include precision, recall and F1.

Unlike accuracy, which evaluates the tagger’s overall performance across all tags, precision, recall and F1 evaluate the tagger’s performance on one particular tag at a time.

Broadly, precision is lowered by false positives. That is, when the tag of a word was predicted, but it is not the gold standard tag.

Recall is lowered by false negatives. That is, when the tag of a word is gold, but it was not predicted. F1 combines the two into a single score.

4.1 Hands-on Exercise

The formulas for precision, recall, and F1 are:

\[\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}\]

\[\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}\]

\[F_1 = \frac{2 \times \text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}\]

where, for a given tag:

  • TP = True Positives (the number of words where the tag is both the gold tag and the predicted tag)
  • FP = False Positives (the number of words where the tag was predicted, but it is not the gold tag)
  • FN = False Negatives (the number of words where the tag is the gold tag, but it was not predicted)
  • TN = True Negatives (the number of words where the tag is not the gold tag, and it is also not the predicted tag)

4.2 Discussion

  1. Why does the tagger get a precision of 1 for VB, but a recall of only \(\frac{1}{3}\)?

VB is likely to get false negatives because these garden path sentences lead us “down the path” to think the first three words are a NP (DET ADJ N), when it is in fact a subject NP + V (DET N V). Garden path sentences therefore have a higher number of VB false negatives than VB false positives, making recall lower than precision.

  1. What are some real life examples where high recall is more important than high precision, and where high precision is more important? It may help to think about when a false positive is the worse mistake, and when a false negative is.

Covid tests require high recall: we would rather risk having a higher number of false positives than false negatives, because we don’t want to risk people walking around if they’ve tested negative when they’re in fact positive.

The criminal justice system requires high precision: we would rather risk having a higher number of false negatives than false positives, because convicting an innocent person is worse than letting a guilty person go free.
